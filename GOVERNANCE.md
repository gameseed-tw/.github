# GameSeed Governance

**Version:** 0.1 — Proposed Draft  
**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** For discussion; **not yet ratified**

[English](GOVERNANCE.md) · [繁體中文](GOVERNANCE.zh-TW.md)

> **Important:** This document is a proposed governance framework for GameSeed's formative stage. It does not establish a legal entity, confer a founder title, appoint any faculty or organization as a partner, transfer intellectual property, or grant spending authority. Its decision rules will take effect only after the founding participants agree to and record their adoption. Until then, existing GitHub permissions and applicable institutional rules continue to govern actual operations.

## 1. Mission and principles

**GameSeed** is an emerging initiative for game-development education, talent cultivation, mentoring, open learning resources, and collaboration. Initiated from the Department of Digital Content Design at Ling Tung University in Taiwan, its long-term aspiration is to connect creators and educators across campuses and borders.

Our guiding framework is **SEED**:

- **Skills:** practical game-development capabilities.
- **Experimentation:** learning through making and testing.
- **Exchange:** cooperation among educators, students, independent developers, and communities.
- **Dissemination:** sharing knowledge and enabling new groups to grow.

We aim for openness, educational benefit, respect for contributors, transparent decision-making, sustainable maintenance, and responsible stewardship of student work and shared resources.

## 2. Organizational status and scope

- GameSeed is **in planning and early development**; it is not represented here as an incorporated organization or an officially established international institution.
- Ling Tung University's Game Development Training Center is the proposed initial practical base. The school's name, facilities, staff time, and resources may only be used within the scope of actual institutional approval.
- Past exchanges with TiGG and other communities do **not** automatically constitute endorsement, an official partnership, or authorization to act on their behalf.
- This policy applies to the GameSeed GitHub organization and shared activities that participating members expressly place under this framework. It does not override laws, employment or academic agreements, school policies, grant terms, or project-specific contracts.

## 3. Participation and public titles

The following are **proposed categories**, not a list of appointed people:

| Capacity | Intended contribution | Automatic legal or GitHub authority? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Founding Initiator / Project Lead** | Steward the original proposal, convene founding discussions, and coordinate the pilot phase. | No |
| **Founding Collaborator / Co-Founder** | Accept explicit joint founding responsibility, help shape governance, and contribute sustained work or resources. | No |
| **Founding Faculty / Academic Collaborator** | Help design curricula, mentor projects, and connect institutions. | No |
| **Steering Committee Member** | Participate in agreed policy and strategic decisions after appointment. | No |
| **Project Maintainer** | Manage scope, review contributions, and maintain a specific repository. | Only permissions explicitly assigned |
| **Mentor / Contributor** | Teach, review, develop, translate, test, or share resources. | No |
| **Partner / Campus Node** | Cooperate through a documented, authorized arrangement. | No |

A **Co-Founder** designation requires the person's informed acceptance and a recorded founding-group decision; it is not inferred from a GitHub invitation, a meeting, or earlier collaboration. The Founding Initiator role recognizes initiative and coordination but does not by itself grant personal ownership of any organization's assets.

Public profiles must list people, institutions, affiliations, and roles **only after confirmation and consent**. Individuals act in their own capacity unless authorized to represent their institutions.

## 4. Proposed governance structure

### 4.1 Interim coordination

Before formal adoption, the initiating coordinator and current authorized GitHub administrators may perform **reversible operational work**: editing clearly labeled drafts, publishing accurate information, maintaining repositories, and arranging discussions. They must not claim unapproved partners, allocate third-party budgets, transfer assets, or bind institutions without authorization.

### 4.2 Steering Committee (to be established)

The founding group may establish a **3–7-person Steering Committee**, with members chosen and recorded by the confirmed participating founders and academic/community collaborators. A proposed term is **one year, renewable**, with regular disclosure of active roles and conflicts of interest. A future committee should seek representation beyond a single institution as the network grows.

### 4.3 Working groups

Project-specific teams may form around curricula, open-source technology, Game Jams, research, mentorship, international exchange, and operations. Each active project should identify at least one maintainer and an appropriate way to contact that team.

## 5. Proposed decision-making process

**The rules below are a proposal, not yet an adopted voting procedure.**

1. **Routine project decisions:** Repository maintainers decide ordinary implementation and review matters in accordance with the repository's own rules.
2. **Cross-project proposals:** Open a written proposal (Issue, Discussion, or version-controlled document), explain alternatives and consequences, and provide **at least seven calendar days** for comments when feasible.
3. **Consensus first:** Seek clear, documented agreement rather than relying on status or informal private messages.
4. **If consensus fails:** For ordinary policy decisions, the proposed fallback is a simple majority of non-recused Steering Committee members voting, with **more than half of non-recused sitting members participating**.
5. **Reserved decisions:** Changes to this charter, formal founder appointments, long-term brand or asset transfers, the admission of official regional chapters, or dissolution require a proposed **two-thirds majority** of non-recused sitting committee members, with at least two-thirds participating, plus any necessary legal and institutional approvals.
6. **Conflicts of interest:** Members disclose relevant financial, institutional, or personal interests and abstain where the decision directly concerns their own appointment, compensation, or related contracts.
7. **Records:** Record the proposal, decision, participants, recusals, date, and follow-up actions. Keep private personal or contractual details out of public repositories.
8. **Urgent incidents:** Authorized administrators may temporarily revoke compromised access or pause a harmful deployment; document the action as soon as practical and submit it for subsequent review.

Where applicable laws, contracts, or school rules require an approval, no committee vote can replace it. Financial commitments cannot be approved solely by this document.

## 6. GitHub stewardship and access

GitHub technical permissions are **separate** from a person's governance title.

- Maintain **at least two trusted Organization Owners** as soon as suitable, mutually accountable people are available, to avoid a single point of failure.
- Enable strong **two-factor authentication** for administrators and contributors; consider an organization-wide requirement after notifying affected members.
- Apply **least privilege**: Read or Triage for discussion/review, Write for active contributors, Maintain for repository managers, and Admin only where necessary.
- Prefer **Teams** for repeatable project access. Give external collaborators repository-specific access when organization membership is unnecessary.
- Protect important branches and require appropriate PR review/checks where supported. Avoid direct changes to critical policies without review after a team is established.
- Review memberships and repository access **at least every six months**, and promptly revoke access when a role ends or a credential may be compromised.
- Keep an inventory of maintainers, repositories, secrets and integrations, domains, and other shared assets in an appropriately access-controlled location. Never store actual secrets in public documents.
- GitHub Owners hold platform administration rights; that does **not** automatically confer copyright, trademark ownership, institutional authority, or a Co-Founder title.

## 7. Repositories, licensing, and attribution

Each project should document its purpose, maintainer(s), development instructions, contribution route, and **its own license**. This governance document is not a blanket license.

- Contributed copyright remains subject to the actual author's rights, applicable educational/employment agreements, and any explicit license or transfer; **publication on GitHub does not automatically transfer ownership to GameSeed**.
- Obtain permission before publishing course materials, student work, research outputs, art, music, code from private projects, or third-party assets.
- Record attribution and license notices for external materials. Avoid presenting reused assets as original.
- Brand names, logos, domains, and accounts require a separately documented ownership and authorized-use arrangement. No collective or personal ownership is asserted by this draft.
- Any commercial use, sponsorship, paid training, or revenue sharing requires a project-specific written agreement and relevant approvals.
- AI-assisted work should be reviewed by a responsible human contributor; disclose material AI involvement when it affects attribution, evaluation, or reproducibility.

## 8. Learning, student protection, and research ethics

- Respect student consent, privacy, educational records, intellectual property, and the boundaries between teaching, research, and commercial work.
- Do not publish identifiable student records, participant responses, private assessment data, credentials, confidential agreements, or unpublished institutional material without proper authority.
- Research activities must follow applicable institutional research-ethics and data-management requirements.
- Participation should not imply guaranteed grades, employment, royalties, scholarships, or professional placement.

## 9. Campus and international network

GameSeed may eventually support regional communities or **campus seed nodes**. A proposed node should have:

1. A named local coordinator and at least one sustainable activity or resource.
2. A transparent proposal describing the institution/community, scope, responsibilities, and branding requests.
3. Written confirmation of any institutional participation or use of the institution's name.
4. Review and approval through the adopted governance process before publicly describing itself as an official GameSeed chapter.

A chapter is **not** automatically a legal branch, franchise, employment arrangement, or representative of Ling Tung University. Joint activities and brand use may require a separate written agreement.

## 10. Funding, transparency, and conflicts

No member may promise financial support, sign contracts, accept sponsorships on behalf of others, or incur liabilities merely because they have a GitHub role or a title in GameSeed.

For any funded activity, identify in writing the **actual legal recipient/contracting party**, authorized approver, budget owner, reporting requirements, and rules for sponsor recognition. Disclose relevant conflicts, safeguard private financial information, and publish non-sensitive progress or activity summaries where practical.

## 11. Disputes, safety, and exit

Participants should communicate respectfully and use the project's appropriate reporting channels. When a concern involves privacy, misconduct, safety, or retaliation, use a confidential authorized contact rather than a public Issue; a formal reporting channel and code of conduct should be agreed upon before broader recruitment.

When a member or maintainer departs, transfer maintainership, remove unnecessary technical access, preserve legally shareable project history, and handle any outstanding rights or obligations according to applicable agreements. Disputes over ownership or employment are not settled by GitHub voting.

## 12. Review and ratification

- Review this draft with proposed founding educators and collaborators before treating it as a governing policy.
- Ratification should record **the actual consenting participants**, the agreed decision rules, the effective date, and any approved amendments.
- After ratification, review governance **at least annually**, and sooner after major changes in institutional arrangements or international expansion.
- Keep English as the primary public text and maintain a Traditional Chinese version. If translations diverge, disclose the difference and resolve it through the adopted amendment process.

### Open questions before ratification

- Who will be confirmed as founding participants and who, if anyone, will hold the title Co-Founder?
- Which institution or legal person may hold the domain, trademarks, funds, and other assets, and on what terms?
- What authority does the Game Development Training Center have under Ling Tung University's internal rules?
- Who will serve as the second trusted GitHub Organization Owner?
- What are the approved partnership, conduct-reporting, security, and publication procedures?
- When and how will the first Steering Committee be constituted?
